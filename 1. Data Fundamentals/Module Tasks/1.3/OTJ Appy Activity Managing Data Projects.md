### Collaboration Strategies and Best Practice

- Align business objectives, success metrics and priorities with leadership and product stakeholders before delivery begins.
- Use Agile ceremonies (planning, stand-ups, reviews and retrospectives) to maintain transparency and gather continuous feedback.
- Engage architecture, engineering and journey SMEs early to identify risks, dependencies and technical constraints.
- Clearly define ownership and responsibilities through a RACI matrix.
- Use dashboards and KPI reporting to ensure decisions are based on measurable outcomes and evidence.

| Stakeholder Group | Engagement Method | Frequency | Purpose |
|------------------|------------------|-----------|---------|
| Leadership & Product | Steering meetings | Monthly | Strategic direction, prioritisation and decision making |

| Architecture & SMEs | Design and solution reviews | As required | Validate designs and identify impacts |
| QA & Testing | Test planning and review sessions | Per sprint | Quality assurance and defect management |

### Key Considerations

- Maintain regular communication channels and agreed reporting structures.
- Manage expectations through transparent progress updates and risk management.
- Escalate blockers promptly using RAID logs.
- Capture decisions and actions in a shared decision log.
- Continuously review stakeholder alignment against business objectives.

# Outcome 2: Championing a Data-Driven Culture Using Agile, Lean and Six Sigma

## Integrating Agile, Lean and Six Sigma

### Agile

Provides:
- Iterative delivery
- Rapid feedback loops
- Flexible prioritisation
- Increased stakeholder engagement

### Lean

Provides:
- Waste reduction
- Improved process flow
- Faster delivery cycles
- Greater focus on customer value

### Six Sigma

Provides:
- Data-driven decision making
- Root-cause analysis
- Reduction in defects and variation
- Process stability and control

### Combined Benefits

Together, Agile, Lean and Six Sigma create a continuous improvement cycle:

**Prioritise → Deliver → Measure → Learn → Improve**

This supports a culture where decisions are driven by evidence, customer outcomes and measurable performance improvements.

---

## Proposal for Implementation

### Step 1: Establish a Pilot

Select a high-value identity journey and establish baseline measurements for:
- Delivery speed
- Defect rates
- Customer outcomes
- Data quality

### Step 2: Form a Cross-Functional Team

Include representatives from:
- Product
- Engineering
- Architecture
- Quality Assurance
- Relevant journey SMEs

### Step 3: Apply Integrated Methodologies

| Methodology | Practices |
|------------|------------|
| Agile | Sprint planning, backlog refinement, reviews, retrospectives |
| Lean | Value stream mapping, waste elimination, process optimisation |
| Six Sigma | DMAIC, root cause analysis, defect reduction and process control |

### Step 4: Share Results

- Demonstrate progress through sprint reviews.
- Publish KPI dashboards.
- Communicate lessons learned and improvement opportunities.

### Step 5: Scale Successful Practices

Expand proven approaches across additional journeys using communities of practice, coaching and standardised processes.

---

## Securing Leadership and Cross-Functional Buy-In

- Present a clear business case linked to strategic objectives.
- Quantify expected benefits, risks and success measures.
- Demonstrate quick wins through pilot outcomes.
- Assign accountable sponsors and owners.
- Maintain regular reporting through dashboards and steering meetings.

---

## Measuring Success

### Business and Customer KPIs

- Customer completion rate
- Customer adoption rate
- Customer satisfaction score
- Benefits delivered

### Delivery KPIs

- Lead time
- Cycle time
- Throughput
- Deployment frequency

### Quality KPIs

- Defect escape rate
- Rework rate
- First-time-right percentage
- Process variation

### Data KPIs

- Data accuracy
- Data completeness
- Data timeliness
- Data quality rule failure rate

### Culture KPIs

- Dashboard usage
- Evidence-based decision adoption
- Continuous improvement participation
- Stakeholder satisfaction
- Cross-functional engagement levels

## Conclusion

By combining Agile, Lean and Six Sigma methodologies, the organisation can establish a sustainable data-driven culture that improves decision-making, increases delivery efficiency, reduces defects and ensures initiatives remain aligned to business objectives. Success depends on strong stakeholder engagement, leadership sponsorship, measurable outcomes and a commitment to continuous improvement.
