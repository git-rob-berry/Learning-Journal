## Collaborate Task

[View the task brief](https://github.com/git-rob-berry/Learning-Journal/blob/fd1ded58a432af1947e665e3fe731407897d3086/1.%20Data%20Fundamentals/Files/M1T1-Collaborate-ActBrief.pdf)

### Suggested Approach

1. Extract data from the CSV, SQL database and JSON sources.
2. Examine the structure, fields and data types in each source.
3. Standardise employee IDs, field names and date formats.
4. Identify missing values, duplicates and inconsistent records.
5. Apply appropriate data-quality rules.
6. Transform the datasets into compatible structures.
7. Merge the sources into a unified HR analytics dataset.
8. Validate the combined dataset.
9. Document assumptions, transformations and limitations.

### Challenges Faced

- Integrating CSV, SQL and JSON data.
- Resolving inconsistent employee identifiers such as `1` and `001`.
- Converting different date formats.
- Identifying missing, duplicated or contradictory records.
- Maintaining data quality and lineage.
- Drawing conclusions from limited sample data.

### Key Insights

- Performance ratings were generally high, ranging from 3.8 to 4.5.
- Engagement scores ranged from 78 to 90.
- Survey feedback indicated strong collaboration and workplace satisfaction.
- Requests for more skill-development opportunities suggested that learning remained a priority.
- Combining performance, learning and engagement data created a fuller view of employee development.

### Example KPIs

- Percentage of employees on track relative to total headcount.
- Relationship between engagement and performance.
- Recommended courses based on comments, review results and engagement scores.
- Employee satisfaction trends.
- Possible relationship between satisfaction and turnover.
- Training completion rates by team.
- Performance changes after development activity.
