### 1. Unlocking data architecture success with diagrams

> Model standardised big data ecosystems and architectures for enterprise data using visual approaches

There are also special types of diagrams commonly used by data engineers, including:

1. **Unified modelling language (UML) diagrams**

2. **Entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs)**

3. **Architecture diagrams (e.g. Layered architecture, microservices architecture)**

**UML - Sequence Diagrams**  

- Sequence diagrams are a type of UML diagram that emphasise the timing of interactions between various components or objects within a system.
- The main objectives of sequence diagrams include: Visualising interactions, Enhancing communication and Validation.

**UML - Use Case Diagrams**  

- A use case diagram is a diagram that provides a visual representation of how users interact with a system. 
- Use case diagrams play a crucial role in system analysis and design by providing a visual representation of user-system interactions.

**Entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs)**  

- A powerful visualisation tool for understanding the relationships between different entities within an application or database.

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/bd30abf0-1c79-49bf-8b20-c8e842986a9b" />
<br><br>

The ERD consists of two main entities: Student and Course. These entities can be broken down as follows:  

Student entity:
- Attributes: Name, Email
- Prime Attribute: SSN

Course entity:  
- Attributes: Title, Duration
- Prime Attribute: Course Identifier (CID)

The relationship between the Student and Course entities is represented by a join relationship in the middle, indicating that a student can enrol in multiple courses, and a course can have multiple students enrolled.  

**Customer Diagrams for Organisational Architecture**  

They can either provide a **logical** overview of data structure across stages, or offer a **solution** or platform-oriented depiction of specific systems data traverses.  

1. **Logical diagram** - Logical diagrams are designed to illustrate the high-level flow of data within our system without referencing any specific vendors. They focus on depicting the structure of data across different stages, providing a conceptual overview of how information moves through our architecture.  
<img width="500" height="auto" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/91a1261e-8421-44c7-b1f4-1a646b3777f7" />
<br><br>

2. **Solution diagram** - In contrast to the logical diagram, a solution diagram provides a more detailed representation of our system architecture, including specific vendor solutions or platforms. It highlights the exact systems and technologies involved at each stage of data processing, offering a more concrete view of our solution landscape.
