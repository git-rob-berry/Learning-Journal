# 5 Examples of Bad Data Quality in Business — And How to Avoid Them

## 1. Unity Technologies: Ad Targeting Error

- **What happened:** Bad customer data entered Unity's Audience Pinpoint system and corrupted the training data used by its predictive ML models.
- **Impact:** Around **$110 million in losses**, delayed product launches, and a **37% fall in share price**.
- **Solution:** Unity introduced stronger **monitoring, alerting, and recovery systems** to detect and respond to future data problems.

## 2. Equifax: Incorrect Credit Scores

- **What happened:** A coding issue on a legacy system caused inaccurate credit scores to be sent to lenders, with over **300,000 people** seeing errors of 20 points or more.
- **Impact:** Some consumers potentially faced incorrect lending decisions; Equifax's shares fell around **5%**, and the company faced a **class-action lawsuit**.
- **Solution:** Equifax accelerated migration from its legacy environment to the **Equifax Cloud**, providing additional monitoring and controls.

## 3. Uber: Driver Payment Miscalculation

- **What happened:** Uber calculated its commission using the **gross fare instead of the net fare**, causing New York drivers to be underpaid by 2.6%.
- **Impact:** Tens of thousands of drivers were affected, with repayments estimated to cost Uber **at least $45 million**.
- **Solution:** Uber **repaid affected drivers**, including **9% annual interest**, with average payments of around $900.

## 4. Samsung Securities: $105 Billion Data Entry Error

- **What happened:** An employee accidentally entered a dividend as **2.8 billion shares rather than 2.8 billion won**, creating approximately $105 billion of erroneous shares.
- **Impact:** Employees sold around $187 million of the erroneous shares before trading was stopped. Samsung Securities subsequently lost around **$300 million in market value**, customers, and the ability to take new clients for six months.
- **Solution:** Samsung stopped employees selling the incorrectly issued shares once the error was discovered. The incident highlighted the importance of controls for detecting unusually large data-entry errors.

## 5. Public Health England: Missing COVID-19 Cases

- **What happened:** An outdated **Excel XLS format** reached its row limit, resulting in **15,841 positive COVID-19 cases** being omitted from reporting.
- **Impact:** More than **50,000 potentially infectious people** may not have been contacted by contact tracers, creating a serious public-health risk.
- **Solution:** PHE planned to **replace the legacy software and move to more suitable data analytics tools** rather than relying on Excel.

# Preventative Measures

## Data Quality Testing

- **Unit testing:** Automatically checks data for expected properties such as freshness, null values, and acceptable value ranges.
- **Integration testing:** Checks that data from different sources is correctly loaded, combined, and transformed according to business rules.
- **Benefit:** Testing data early helps catch known problems **before they reach downstream systems and users**.

## Agent Observability

- Monitors **AI agents and their outputs** for quality problems.
- Helps detect the effects of changes to **prompts, models, agent code, and other components**.
- Poor-quality input data can result in unreliable AI outputs, making monitoring particularly important.

## Data Governance

- Establishes clear policies for **how data is collected, stored, accessed, processed, protected, and retired**.
- Defines **data ownership and accountability**, ensuring responsibility for data is clear.
- Creates agreed data-quality standards covering areas such as **accuracy, completeness, and consistency**.

## Data Observability

- Continuously monitors data across the **end-to-end data stack**, helping identify known and unexpected problems.
- Automatically alerts teams to issues such as **inaccurate, erroneous, stale, or missing data**.
- Uses techniques such as **data lineage** to trace where problems originated and identify downstream systems that may be affected.

---

# Key Takeaways

- **Test** data to catch expected problems.
- **Govern** data so ownership, standards, and rules are clear.
- **Observe** data continuously to catch unexpected problems quickly.
- **Monitor AI agents** so changes to data, models, prompts, or code do not silently reduce the quality of their outputs.
